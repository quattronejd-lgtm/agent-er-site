Why is my Claude project not following its instructions?

Short answer: A Claude project usually stops following its instructions because the instructions have grown long and contradict each other, or because a long chat has buried them under newer messages. The fix is to trim the project instructions to a short list of clear rules, put the most important rule in your actual message, and start a fresh chat once a conversation gets long.

Why this happens

Project instructions work like background guidance. Claude reads them and tries to follow them, but there is no guarantee every rule wins every time, especially when rules are vague or conflict.

In a long chat, your instructions become a smaller and smaller share of everything Claude is reading. Users and even bug reports describe Claude following the rules at first and then slowly slipping back to its default habits.

Adding one more line every time something goes wrong makes this worse, because the list gets longer and the lines start to disagree.

How to fix it, step by step

  1. Open your project instructions and copy them somewhere safe as a backup.
  2. Cut them down to the ten or fewer rules that truly matter.
  3. Make each rule testable. "No paragraph longer than three sentences" is easier to follow than "be concise."
  4. Remove any rule that conflicts with another one, including rules in your profile settings.
  5. When a rule really matters for a task, repeat it in the message you send, not only in the project settings.
  6. End important requests with "Before you answer, list which of my rules apply here."
  7. When a chat gets long or starts slipping, ask Claude for a short summary of decisions so far, then paste it into a new chat.

Common questions

Why does Claude follow my rules at first and then stop?

As the conversation grows, the instructions become a smaller part of what Claude is paying attention to. Starting a fresh chat with a short summary usually restores the behavior.

Should I ask Claude why it broke a rule?

It usually does not help. Claude will often give an apology that sounds reasonable but does not explain the real cause, and that back and forth uses up space in the conversation. Restate the rule and ask for a redo instead.

Do project files work the same as instructions?

No. Project files are reference material. Rules about how Claude should behave belong in the project instructions and, for critical tasks, in your message.

How many rules should my project instructions have?

There is no official number, but many users report better results after cutting their instructions to a short list of clear, non-conflicting rules.
