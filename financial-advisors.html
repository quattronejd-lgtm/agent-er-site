The guardrails we design in

FINRA said in Regulatory Notice 24-09 that its rules are technology neutral and keep applying when firms use generative AI. So the question is never only whether an agent works. It is whether the way you use it still fits your firm's rules. We build around four common areas.

1. Communications and recordkeeping

FINRA's books and records guidance says business communications, including chat messages, must be captured and preserved. Our agents are set up to draft messages for you to send through your firm's approved, archived channels, rather than sending client messages on their own through tools your firm does not capture.

2. Approval of marketing content

FINRA has said the content standards of Rule 2210 apply whether a communication is written by a person or by a technology tool. Our agents label marketing drafts as drafts and remind you to route them through your firm's review and approval process before anything is posted or sent.

3. No personalized investment recommendations

We do not build agents that tell clients what to buy, sell, or hold. Agents we set up can summarize, organize, and draft, and they are instructed to leave investment advice to you, the licensed professional.

4. Keeping client information safe

Client personal information should only go into tools your firm has approved. Our setups are designed to keep client names, account numbers, and other sensitive data out of unapproved tools, and we ask you to remove that data from any files you send us. LPL's published Responsible AI Principles are a good example of the kind of expectations a firm may set, including human review of AI-generated content and safeguarding protected information.

Common questions from advisors

Does FINRA allow financial advisors to use AI agents?

FINRA has not banned AI. In Regulatory Notice 24-09, FINRA said its rules are technology neutral and continue to apply when member firms use generative AI, just as they apply to any other tool. Whether you personally may use a given tool depends on your firm's policies, so check with your compliance team first.

What is FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09?

Regulatory Notice 24-09, published June 27, 2024, reminds member firms of their existing obligations when they use generative AI and large language models. It says the Notice does not create new requirements, and it points to areas such as supervision under Rule 3110 and the content standards of Rule 2210 for communications.

Do I have to keep records of AI chats and AI-written messages?

FINRA's guidance says business communications such as emails, instant messages, and chat messages must be captured and preserved under SEC Rule 17a-4(b)(4) and FINRA rules, and its 2025 oversight report says chatbot sessions used with investors should be supervised and retained. A Skadden analysis notes that AI output that is never sent may not be a required communication, while AI-generated content that is sent by email or chat likely is. Ask your compliance team how your firm applies this.

Can an AI agent write my marketing posts and client emails?

An agent can help draft them, but FINRA has said the content standards of Rule 2210 apply whether a communication is written by a person or a technology tool. That means AI drafts still go through your firm's normal review and approval process before they are published or sent.

Can an AI agent give my clients investment recommendations?

We do not build agents that make personalized investment recommendations. Recommendations carry suitability and best interest obligations that belong with you and your firm, so the agents we set up draft, organize, and summarize, and leave advice to the licensed professional.

Is it safe to put client information into ChatGPT or Claude?

Only if your firm has approved that tool for client data. Many firms restrict which AI tools may touch client information. We design setups that keep client personal information out of unapproved tools and ask you to remove sensitive data from files you send us.

Does LPL Financial let advisors use AI tools?

LPL has published a curated list of pre-approved AI tools for advisors and a set of Responsible AI Principles that call for human review of AI-generated content and protection of client information. If you are affiliated with LPL, check its current approved tool list and your branch compliance contact before adopting any new tool.
